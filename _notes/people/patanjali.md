# Patanjali

> Ancient Indian scholar traditionally credited with three monumental works purifying the mind, speech, and body.

A famous Sanskrit verse captures the traditional attribution:

> *yogena chittasya padena vacham, malam sharirasya cha vaidyakena* — He purified the mind through Yoga, speech through Grammar, and the body through Medicine.

## Three Great Works

### 1. Yoga Sutras

The **Yoga Sutras** is a foundational text of Raja Yoga, consisting of 196 sutras organized into four chapters (padas): Samadhi, Sadhana, Vibhuti, and Kaivalya. It systematizes the philosophy and practice of yoga, introducing the eightfold path (Ashtanga Yoga). This is one of the most influential texts in Indian philosophy and is widely studied and practiced to this day.

### 2. Mahabhashya (Grammar)

The **Mahabhashya** ("Great Commentary") is a detailed commentary on [[Panini]]'s Ashtadhyayi and Katyayana's Vartikas. It is a landmark work in Sanskrit grammar and linguistics. The grammarian Patanjali is dated to approximately **150 BCE** and is one of the most historically well-documented figures bearing this name.

### 3. Contribution to Ayurveda

Patanjali is traditionally credited with a contribution to Ayurveda (Indian medicine), often identified as a revision or annotation of the **Charaka Samhita** (sometimes called the Charakapratisamskrita). This attribution is the least well-documented of the three and rests more on tradition than on strong textual evidence.

## Historical Identity

Modern Indological scholarship generally considers that the three works attributed to Patanjali were likely authored by **different individuals** across different centuries:

- The **grammarian** Patanjali (Mahabhashya) is most confidently dated to around **150 BCE**.
- The **Yoga Sutras** are variously dated between the **2nd century BCE and 4th century CE**, with most scholars favoring around the 2nd century BCE to 2nd century CE.
- The **Ayurvedic** attribution has the least independent historical support.

The tradition of a single Patanjali authoring all three works is a respected belief in Indian culture, but the conflation likely arose from later hagiographic tradition rather than historical evidence.

## Legacy

Patanjali — whether one person or several — represents a towering contribution to Indian intellectual life across philosophy, linguistics, and medicine. The Yoga Sutras remain a living text practiced worldwide, and the Mahabhashya is still essential reading for students of Sanskrit grammar.
